Project 001: ED-to-Inpatient Pathways

Who is admitted from the emergency department, how long do they stay, and what changes with asthma?

From Emergency Department to Inpatient Care: Admission, Length of Stay and Asthma-Related Risk in Simulated Alberta Administrative Data.

An end-to-end health data science project on one shared synthetic dataset: 30,000 Alberta-style emergency visits (NACRS) and 60,000 inpatient abstracts (DAD) across 30 hospitals.

Ten memos take it from raw extract to answers. They ask what is linked to admission, link admitted visits to their inpatient stays to ask what is linked to length of stay, then build a fair comparison for adults with asthma, by hospital and by region, using propensity-score matching, outcome regression and mixed models. Because the true effects are built into the data, the project also checks whether those methods recover them.

Read the project overview See the ten memos

Every patient record is simulated. The 30 hospital names are real Alberta emergency departments used as reference labels only; no result describes how those hospitals actually perform.

Four questions, four answers

Headline results from the project summary, Memo A09. Every figure comes from simulated data.

Full project overview

Q1 · Admission · A03

Triage level drives admission

The adjusted odds of admission fall at every CTAS level, to an odds ratio of 0.056 at CTAS 5 versus CTAS 1. Time before the disposition decision adds little: OR 1.024 per 60 minutes (95% CI 1.003 to 1.045).

Q2 · Stay in hospital · A04–A05

ED time does not predict inpatient stay

4,633 of 5,810 admitted visits (79.7%) link to an inpatient stay. Once CTAS is included, ED stay is unrelated to inpatient stay (−0.011 days per 60 minutes, 95% CI −0.056 to 0.034). Stays after a CTAS 5 visit are 2.78 days shorter than after CTAS 1.

Q3 · Asthma · A06–A07

Asthma adds about 10 points of admission risk

Across 7,700 within-hospital matched pairs, admission is 31.8% versus 21.8%: +10.00 points (95% CI 7.59 to 12.41). Outcome regression on the same pairs gives +9.54 points (8.47 to 10.61). The value built into the simulation is 10.38.

Q4 · Regions · A08

The asthma gap differs by region

With a common patient mix, Edmonton-region hospitals admit adult asthma visits more often than Other (+7.23 points, 95% CI 2.16 to 12.99); Calgary's −5.72 points is not established. The asthma gap differs by region (χ² = 78.0 on 2 df, p < 0.001).

Do the methods work? Across repeated simulations with a known truth, the asthma estimators are biased by at most 0.61 points, 95% intervals cover the truth 92%–98% of the time, and false-positive rates are 3%–4% when there is no effect. Read the A09 summary

Project 002: Calgary Housing Observatory

What does Calgary's housing market look like, community by community?

An interactive R Shiny dashboard on 21,279 Calgary residential listings, matched to the City of Calgary's official community boundaries. Filter by price, home type, bedrooms and year built, and the community map, charts and table all update together.

The spread is far wider between communities than between sectors: among communities with at least 50 listings, median list prices run from about $272,000 in Lincoln Park to $2.26 million in Elbow Park.

A notebook for health data science

I'm Miss V. The V Lab is where I learn health data science properly and then use it: each method gets a long-form guide first, and then a place in a project built on realistic Alberta administrative data.

Everything here is synthetic or public. The data designs follow the official documentation for datasets like NACRS and DAD, and every page and analysis is open on GitHub.
